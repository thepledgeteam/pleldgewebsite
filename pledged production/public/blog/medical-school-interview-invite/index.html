You’ve Received a Medical School Interview Invite—Now What?

Published on: September 2025

Receiving a medical school interview invitation is a major milestone. It usually means the admissions committee sees you as a strong candidate and wants to evaluate you beyond grades and test scores.

After the excitement settles, many applicants ask the same question: What should I do next?

What Medical Schools Are Assessing

Medical school interviews are competency-based. In Canada, schools rely heavily on the CanMEDS framework. In the United States, the equivalent is the ACGME Core Competencies.

  • Medical Expert / Medical Knowledge
  • Communicator
  • Collaborator
  • Leader / Manager
  • Health Advocate (Canada)
  • Scholar
  • Professional

If you’ve been invited to interview, your application has already demonstrated early evidence of these traits. The interview explores them further.

Types of Medical School Interviews

Most schools use either traditional interviews or Multiple Mini Interviews (MMIs).

University of Toronto: Modified Personal Interview (MPI)

The University of Toronto uses a four-station MPI, with each station assessing different non-academic domains such as professionalism, communication, advocacy, and scholarship.

How to Answer Interview Questions Strategically

Your goal is not to impress with accomplishments, but to demonstrate insight, reflection, and growth.

Example: “What is your greatest achievement?”

Choose an experience that allows you to demonstrate multiple competencies—teamwork, leadership, advocacy, and learning. Focus on what you learned and how it shaped you.

Interview Preparation Does Not Need to Be Expensive

Practicing with family, friends, or mentors can be extremely effective once you understand what schools are assessing.

While coaching can help, you do not need to pay exorbitant fees. A focused session with an experienced admissions coach is often more than enough.

Final Thought: Preparation is about clarity and authenticity—not memorized answers.
